Where Pose Estimation Is Going

More Robust Real-Time Estimation

Future systems can continue improving landmark stability under challenging conditions such as occlusion, unusual viewpoints, and fast movement.

Richer 3D Understanding

Improved 3D pose estimation can provide more information about how the body moves through space rather than relying only on positions projected into a 2D image.

Exercise, Sports, and Rehabilitation

Pose estimation can support applications that analyze human movement, including fitness, sports analysis, and rehabilitation-related tools. It does not replace professional assessment.

Conclusion

Human pose estimation converts images or video into body landmarks. These landmarks provide a structured representation of the body that downstream algorithms can use.

Different systems such as BlazePose, YOLO Pose, and OpenPose use different approaches and are suited to different application requirements. In practice, the right choice depends on whether the goal is low-latency tracking, high-detail body keypoints, or a different balance of speed and flexibility.

For exercise analysis, landmarks can be converted into measurements such as joint angles and movement states. The squat example showed how hip, knee, and ankle coordinates can be turned into a knee-angle calculation and used as part of a repetition-counting workflow.

Real-world performance remains affected by camera angle, occlusion, lighting, motion, and other capture conditions. When those factors vary, the estimated landmarks should be treated as approximate measurements rather than perfect body positions.
