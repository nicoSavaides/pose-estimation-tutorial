Annotated Bibliography

The sources below support the technical concepts, systems, and exercise-analysis applications discussed throughout the tutorial.

Listen to Page Overview

[1] Miniar Ben Gamra and Moulay A. Akhloufi

“A Review of Deep Learning Techniques for 2D and 3D Human Pose Estimation.” Image and Vision Computing, Volume 114, 2021, Article 104282. DOI: 10.1016/j.imavis.2021.104282.

Source link

Synopsis: This review surveys deep-learning approaches to both 2D and 3D human pose estimation, including different architectural categories and the challenges associated with single-person and multi-person pose estimation. It provides broad background for the tutorial's explanation of pose estimation and its major approaches.

[2] Ivan Grishchenko, Valentin Bazarevsky, Andrei Zanfir, Eduard Gabriel Bazavan, Mihai Zanfir, Richard Yee, Karthik Raveendran, Matsvei Zhdanovich, Matthias Grundmann, and Cristian Sminchisescu

“BlazePose GHUM Holistic: Real-time 3D Human Landmarks and Pose Estimation.” 2022. arXiv:2206.11678.

Source link

Synopsis: The paper presents BlazePose GHUM Holistic, a lightweight pipeline designed for real-time, on-device 3D human landmark and pose estimation from RGB imagery. The work specifically identifies fitness tracking among its applications and supports this tutorial's discussion of BlazePose for real-time exercise analysis.

[3] Ultralytics

“Pose Estimation with Ultralytics YOLO.” Ultralytics Documentation.

Source link

Synopsis: The official documentation describes how Ultralytics YOLO performs pose estimation and exposes predicted keypoint coordinates. It documents access to pixel and normalized keypoint coordinates and the standard 17-keypoint COCO representation used by pretrained human pose models.

[4] Zhe Cao, Tomas Simon, Shih-En Wei, and Yaser Sheikh

“Realtime Multi-Person 2D Pose Estimation Using Part Affinity Fields.” Proceedings of the IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2017. DOI: 10.1109/CVPR.2017.143.

Source link

Synopsis: The paper introduces a bottom-up multi-person 2D pose-estimation method using Part Affinity Fields to associate detected body parts with individual people. It supports the tutorial's discussion of OpenPose and illustrates an approach that differs from single-person landmark pipelines.

[5] Yu Cheng Hsu, Tsougenis Efstratios, and Kwok-Leung Tsui

“Viewpoint-invariant exercise repetition counting.” Health Information Science and Systems, Volume 12, Article 1, 2024. DOI: 10.1007/s13755-023-00258-3.

Source link

Synopsis: This research investigates vision-based exercise repetition counting using skeleton information obtained from pose estimation and considers challenges including different camera viewpoints and concurrent motions. It provides an application-focused source connecting pose-estimation output with exercise repetition counting.
